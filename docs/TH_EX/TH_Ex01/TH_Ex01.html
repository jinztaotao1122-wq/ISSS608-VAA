Overview

This report investigates a real-world motor vehicle insurance portfolio from Spain to identify the main drivers of portfolio profitability and volatility. Following the take-home brief, the analysis uses tidyverse for data processing and ggplot2 plus its extensions for visual analytics.

The core business questions are:

  1. Which policy, customer and vehicle segments are associated with weaker profitability?
  2. Which segments show higher claim volatility through elevated claim frequency or claim severity?
  3. What actions should an insurer prioritise to improve portfolio performance?

Data Preparation

Wrangling Workflow

The analytical sandbox was prepared with the following steps:

  1. Import the semicolon-delimited CSV using read_delim().
  2. Validate the 47 variables against the accompanying codebook.
  3. Derive business metrics required by the brief:
    • loss_ratio = total_incurred / total_premium
    • claim_frequency = total_claims / total_exposure
    • claim_severity = total_incurred / total_claims
    • premium_per_exposure, pure_premium, total_margin
    • age and value bands for interpretable segmentation
  4. Cap loss_ratio and claim_frequency at the 99th percentile for distribution plots, while keeping uncapped values for aggregated portfolio metrics.
  5. Retain the 49 records with zero premium and zero exposure in the base data, but exclude them automatically from ratio calculations to avoid infinite results.

Executive Summary of Findings

Four messages stand out from the analysis:

  1. Comprehensive products drive the weakest profitability. COMP_N has the highest claim frequency and loss ratio, and its loss ratio exceeded 1.0 in 2024.
  2. Portfolio renewals underperform new business. The existing portfolio segment shows consistently higher claim incidence, claim frequency and loss ratio than new business.
  3. Billing cadence and underwriting quality matter. Semiannual and quarterly payment plans perform worse than annual plans, while favourable bonus scores materially reduce claim risk.
  4. The portfolio deteriorated in 2024. Loss ratio rose to roughly 74.7% in 2024 from about 65.6% in 2022, indicating worsening profitability despite only modest movement in claim severity.

Higher vehicle values are associated with weaker profitability across most age bands. Younger drivers have the highest claim frequency, but the broader profitability problem is not limited to youth; it also appears in large mature segments exposed to high-value vehicles.

Recommendations

Based on the visual analytics evidence, the insurer should prioritise five actions:

  1. Reprice or redesign comprehensive covers, especially COMP_N, because they combine weak profitability with high volatility.
  2. Review portfolio renewals more closely than new business, since the existing portfolio carries worse frequency and profitability outcomes.
  3. Promote annual billing and review surcharges on instalment plans, because quarterly and semiannual billing underperform.
  4. Use bonus score more aggressively in underwriting and retention, as favourable claims history is one of the clearest risk separators.
  5. Drill into the 2024 deterioration, especially within comprehensive products and high-value vehicles, to distinguish pricing drift from claims inflation.

Conclusion

This take-home exercise shows how visual analytics helps move beyond dashboard reporting into portfolio discovery. The portfolio is dominated by a few product lines, and those product lines do not perform equally. Comprehensive covers, portfolio renewals, non-annual billing, weak bonus scores and higher-value vehicles all contribute to lower profitability or higher volatility. Just as importantly, the deterioration observed in 2024 suggests that the insurer should treat the problem as dynamic rather than static.

## Recommendations

Based on the visual analytics evidence, the insurer should prioritise five actions:

1. **Reprice or redesign comprehensive covers**, especially `COMP_N`, because they combine weak profitability with high volatility.
2. **Review portfolio renewals more closely than new business**, since the existing portfolio carries worse frequency and profitability outcomes.
3. **Promote annual billing and review surcharges on instalment plans**, because quarterly and semiannual billing underperform.
4. **Use bonus score more aggressively in underwriting and retention**, as favourable claims history is one of the clearest risk separators.
5. **Drill into the 2024 deterioration**, especially within comprehensive products and high-value vehicles, to distinguish pricing drift from claims inflation.

## Conclusion

This take-home exercise shows how visual analytics helps move beyond dashboard reporting into portfolio discovery. The portfolio is dominated by a few product lines, and those product lines do not perform equally. Comprehensive covers, portfolio renewals, non-annual billing, weak bonus scores and higher-value vehicles all contribute to lower profitability or higher volatility. Just as importantly, the deterioration observed in 2024 suggests that the insurer should treat the problem as dynamic rather than static.
